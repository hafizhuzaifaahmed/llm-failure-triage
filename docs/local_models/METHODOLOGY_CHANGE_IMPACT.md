# Methodology Change Impact — proprietary/API models → local open-weight models (PLANNING ONLY)

This document states what changes if the study replaces the Phase 2 model roles (M1 DeepSeek-V3-family,
M2 Qwen3-family, G GPT-4.1, all via API) with local open-weight models served by Ollama on one laptop GPU,
at zero API cost. It is a proposal for the authors; nothing here has been executed.

**No equivalence is claimed.** The local judges (9–12 B parameters, or ≈ 4 B active) are far smaller than
DeepSeek-V3 and are quantized; results with them say nothing directly about DeepSeek-V3-family, Qwen3-family or GPT-4.1 models.

## 3. External validity
- **Model scale.** The base papers used much larger models: JSTestCraft voted with GPT-4, DeepSeek-V3 and Qwen3;
  CANDOR used Llama 3.1 70B and a 70B distillation; Zhao et al. evaluated 11 LLMs in 13 configurations. Accuracy
  levels from 9–12 B quantized judges cannot be compared with those papers' numbers, which were already
  non-comparable for other reasons.
- **What may still transfer.** The *relative* questions — does Javadoc/spec help (RQ2), does debate beat
  equal-budget self-consistency (RQ3), does LLM triage beat a failure-location rule (RQ1) — are asked
  within one model. Whether the answers transfer to larger models is an open question, and E5-R3 (two local
  families) is the only cross-model check.
- **Practical relevance.** Small local models are what many teams can actually run on CI hardware; this is a
  legitimate deployment setting, and the paper may say so without claiming it represents frontier models.
- **Generator.** Tests generated by a local MoE model may differ in style and fault-revealing rate from GPT-4.1
  tests, changing the PF/TF mix; the P1 gate and full reporting of yields address this.
- **Contamination.** Training-data cut-offs of the local models are later than Defects4J's public history; D2
  (new faults) remains the contamination control.

## 4. Deviations to report in the paper
1. All three model roles replaced by local open-weight models (L-1–L-3), for zero cost; no equivalence claimed.
2. G and M2 share a model family (L-3).
3. G chosen on the development split by pre-specified criteria (L-10).
4. Effective input limit 7,680 tokens under `num_ctx = 8192` (L-4).
5. Thinking disabled and verified (L-8); quantized weights (exact quantization reported).
6. Budget expressed as GPU time (L-6); provider pinning replaced by digest pinning (L-7).
7. If any judge required a smaller quantization or the E4B fallback to stay GPU-resident, that is reported.
These add to the existing register (X-01 … X-09); X-01 and D-18 (OpenRouter provider pins) would become moot.

## 5. Phase 2 decisions that hold only conditionally
- **P2 gate (0.2 ≤ J1 F1 ≤ 0.9) and G-d (beat KA's 0.50 at 1:2 prevalence)**: small judges may fail them. The
  protocol then stops for the authors rather than switching models silently.
- **Spec Inferencer = M1** (Phase 2 §3.6.1): kept, so S2 quality depends on the small model; the leakage audit
  remains essential.
- **Token-matched SC-T**: k_T is computed from the local model's measured tokens; tokens are not comparable
  across models, which Phase 2 never required.
