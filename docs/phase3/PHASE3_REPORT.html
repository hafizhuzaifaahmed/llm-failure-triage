Research questions. RQ1 accuracy against buggy/fixed truth and bug-revealing tests kept; RQ2 specification level; RQ3 debate against self-consistency at equal budget. 2. Dataset, preprocessing and experimental setup

D1 frame (Defects4J 3.0.1). Zhao et al.'s selection rule was re-run with their replication package: 1,758 candidate methods screened, 312 focal methods from 220 defects kept (the original reports 318 / 233); the per-project cap leaves 234 methods from 167 defects. Project split (seeded, before any generation): development 39 methods (Cli, Gson, Jsoup); held-out 195 methods from 14 projects.

Generation, execution, labels. Three generations per focal method by G. Across both D1 splits 2,909 test methods were executed: 2,284 passed on the buggy version, 102 were misguided, 518 did not compile on one version, 6 timed out. Held-out labels: 60 PF, 296 TF, 13 AMB.

Evaluation samples. All PF kept; TF sampled to twice the PF count over the split, stratified by project, with inverse-probability weights. Judged D1-test sample (counts): 60 PF, 120 TF, 13 AMB. Leakage-guard aborts at S1: 4 instances, excluded from every arm at that level (D-70) → 177 scored instances at S1 (PF prevalence 0.339). D2 (seeded faults): 284 mutants → 468 PF, 349 TF, 65 AMB; judged sample capped at 300 (159 PF, 119 TF, 22 AMB), 270 scored; PF prevalence 0.570. D3 (GitBug-Java) not run (needs Docker).

Models (all local, zero API cost). Ollama 0.40.13 on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti Laptop GPU (12,227 MiB VRAM, 31 GB RAM). G = gemma4:26b, M1 = qwen3.5:9b, M2 = gemma4:12b, all Q4_K_M, thinking off, frozen before any held-out call (D-57). Judges: temperature 0.7, top-p 1.0, context 8,192 tokens, output ≤ 512 tokens, per-call seed, 3 replicates for E1–E3; SC = 5 samples; k_T = 5. Generator selection on dev: reliability 0.595 (CI [0.445, 0.733]), assertion validity 0.725, PF yield 5.0%. M1 development gate: one-shot F1 0.350 (MCC −0.032) vs R0 0.642 (MCC 0.363) on 55 instances / 12 bugs — failed, reported, M1 kept (D-48–D-52). Non-determinism: 0% label flips over 49 repeated judgements.

Statistics. Per-replicate metrics averaged; bug-level cluster bootstrap (10,000 resamples) for CIs and paired differences; five confirmatory F1 tests Holm-corrected; equivalence margin ±0.05 F1 (90% CI).

5. Comparison with existing research

Only JSTestCraft reports a triage metric (audited recall 68.75%*); JavaOracle reports a precision of 0.889* (* = taken from the paper, not comparable: other language and data, audit-based labels, different prevalence, larger models). On identical D1-test instances, DEB's F1 exceeds the re-implemented rule baseline R0 by +0.245 (relative +92.3%), SC (the single-model analogue of JSTestCraft's vote) by +0.074 and keep-all by +0.004; none is significant after Holm. DEB's recall (0.500) and precision (0.523) are below the two published figures. No improvement over a base paper can be computed, because no base paper reports a triage result on this data, metric or label source. Our numbers differ from the base papers in language, label source (execution, not an audit), judge size (E9 is the frontier reference), task and metric, and prevalence; where the designs meet they agree: the effective-test interval overlaps Zhao et al.'s range (their package carries no generated tests to re-run, D-98), a cross-family vote keeps recall high (0.900) as JSTestCraft's does, and debate trades away true positives as JavaOracle's does. E7b (GitBug-Java) was not run: first its setup exceeded the free disk (D-101), and once disk was freed its premise failed, because its bugs date from 2023 while every model here is a 2026 release, so it cannot test memorisation (D-103). E7a (a second generator from a third family, D-100) was not run either, because neither candidate passed the D-22 selection on dev: phi4 14B executed 0.378 [0.270, 0.490] of its generated methods (C1, threshold 0.50; C2 0.555, P1 0.136), llama3.1 8B 0.166 [0.099, 0.241] with C2 0.359 [0.210, 0.519]. H7-1 and H7-2 therefore stay untested; the generator remains a threat to external validity. The re-implemented three-family vote (E8, the closest analogue of JSTestCraft's three-LLM vote) reached F1 0.614 against SC3's 0.463 (+0.150 [0.024, 0.279], p Holm 0.063).

Disclosed harness faults. The E6b gate script crashed on a leakage-abort bookkeeping gap before any gate value was produced (fixed, D-86); a unit test made 17 unplanned calls by a non-selected candidate on D1-test, quarantined unread (D-87); the generation-truncation check (zero calls) found 5 of 702 generations (0.7%) cut off by length, so E7a keeps the frozen context (D-91); the G-selection script crashed on 2 llama3.1 classes with unbalanced braces and was fixed, counting them as generated but not executed, before any of that candidate's values was computed (D-102).

Keep unless unanimous, KUU (D-95; proposed design principle). Discard a failure only when one J1 call each from M1, M2 and E6b all say test fault; keep it otherwise (including on an invalid vote). No tuned parameter: debate's confidence-100 rule set its threshold on the development split, KUU fits nothing to data. It is a safety rule, not a better classifier. In JSTestCraft's vote a majority out-votes a dissenting judge; unanimity treats dissent as a reason to keep. KUU was defined after the D1 vote (post hoc on D1) and tested once on unseen D2 under D-95. On D1 (post hoc: F1 0.557, 1 of 60 bug-revealing tests lost). Confirmatory on D2 with fresh calls (270 instances, 64 bugs): F1 0.771 [0.686, 0.837] against keep-all's 0.726 — H10-1 ΔF1 +0.045 [0.005, 0.096], p Holm 0.019, supported; 4 of 154 bug-revealing tests lost against M1 DEB's 56 — H10-2 p Holm <0.001, supported; 13% of failures discarded; MCC 0.356 against DEB's 0.392.

Frontier reference point, not a protocol replication (E9; D-92, D-97). Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Message Batches API on the 177 scored instances (J1 one replicate, DEB three; default sampling, no seeds; spend US$ 13.11): J1 F1 0.745, DEB F1 0.823 (MCC 0.734); H9-1 DEB − J1 +0.077 [0.003, 0.176] (p Holm 0.040) and H9-2 supported; Sonnet − M1 for DEB +0.312 [0.176, 0.441]. DEB's run-to-run F1 range is 0.024 for Sonnet against 0.221 for M1. Sonnet's training data very likely include Defects4J and its fixes, so its D1 score may be inflated by memorisation; E9b (D-104) tested this on the D2 seeded faults (below).

Replicate noise (D-94, exploratory). M1's F1 range across its three replicates is 0.042 for J1, 0.065 for SC and 0.221 for DEB, so every single-replicate DEB difference across judges lies within M1's own noise, while the J1, SC and vote-versus-SC differences exceed it.

Keep unless unanimous with disagreement escalation (D-105; the method). Tier 1 is KUU's three cross-family J1 votes. A unanimous TF vote discards and a unanimous PF vote keeps. Any disagreement, including an invalid vote, goes to Sonnet's DEB, whose valid label decides; a missing answer keeps the test. The cascade and its hypotheses were committed (D-105) before any frontier call on D2, so its D2 result is confirmatory and its D1 result post hoc.

*Error diversity, fixed in advance.* On D2, two families miss the same PF test 2.1 times as often as independence predicts. Three samples of one model do so 7.7 times as often (difference [−19.19, −2.55]), and KUU lost 4 tests where same-model unanimity would have lost 18 (M1) and 11 (M2). The diversity is partial: independence predicts 0.62 losses. On D1 (post hoc) the criterion fixed in advance is not met, because KUU's single loss ties M2's.

*Cascade on D2.*

  • 134 of 270 instances escalated (50%), so the other half of the failures never left the machine (100% leave for any frontier-everywhere option). Per instance: local tier 8.0 GPU-seconds on one laptop GPU, frontier tier US$ 0.011; Sonnet DEB on every instance US$ 0.024. No cost-dominance is claimed.
  • H12-1 is supported. MCC was 0.666 against KUU's 0.356 (Δ +0.311 [0.202, 0.460], p Holm <0.001).
  • H12-2 is not supported. F1 was 0.863 against Sonnet DEB on every instance at 0.910 (Δ −0.046, 95% CI [−0.081, −0.018]). Its lower bound falls below the −0.05 margin, so non-inferiority is not shown.
  • PF tests lost: 15 (KUU 4, Sonnet 13, M1 DEB 56).
  • D1 (post hoc): MCC 0.595, F1 0.741 against Sonnet's 0.823.

Memorisation check, E9b (D-104). Sonnet ran on the 270 D2 instances, one replicate each of J1 and DEB, spending US$ 7.65 with no refusals.

Sensitivity analyses requested by two mock reviews (D-106, post hoc). These are zero-call analyses, fixed before computing; none re-reads a verdict.

  • MCC family. On MCC no confirmatory test survives Holm, the same verdict as F1.
  • Two-level bootstrap. Resampling bugs and replicates widens the confirmatory intervals by up to 1.6×.
  • Matched operating point. M1 DEB at confidence 100 discards 14% and loses 6 bug-revealing tests. KUU discards 13% and loses 4.
  • Cost frontier. Sonnet J1 on every instance (US$ 1.18, MCC 0.687) is cheaper than the cascade at a similar MCC, but leaves more bugs uncovered (7 against 2).
  • Deployment prevalence. At a 5% bug-revealing share, the cascade's precision would be 0.160.
  • Leave-one-project-out. H1, H3a and H3b never change sign.
  • Leave-one-judge-out (D-107). Without M2, the M1 + E6b pair loses 16 bug-revealing tests on D2 against KUU's 4, so much of KUU's protection comes from M2, which shares the generator's family. It still loses far fewer than M1 DEB (56). KUU-NG (above) then tested the question with a third family in M2's place.

The triage of every objection is in docs/paper/MOCK_REVIEW.md.

Findings. (1) Failure evidence carries the signal (A4). (2) DEB has the highest MCC in every dataset × judge combination, but H3a is not significant (p Holm 0.532), and by F1 SC is higher than DEB under M1 on D2 (+0.008). (3) Javadoc does not help and trends negative (H2a −0.050, p Holm 0.116). The larger judge M2 improved every arm. The development gate (one-shot F1 below R0's) did not predict the held-out result (one-shot F1 0.460 vs R0 0.266).

Gaps and objectives. G1 (accuracy against buggy/fixed truth): measured; confirmatory effects inconclusive at 177 instances. G2 (specification): no gain from Javadoc or inferred specifications. G3 (equal budget): small, non-significant debate advantage; SC does not beat J1. RQ4: KUU cut lost bug-revealing tests without lowering F1, with or without a generator-family judge; escalation recovered MCC but not the frontier judge's F1.

Limitations. Small sample; small local judges instead of the planned API models; M2 shares G's family (a third-family judge only partly offsets this); D3 not run; the label audit covers development-split instances only (D-108: 71 instances, human–human κ 0.936 [0.638, 1.000], consensus vs execution label 97.2%, both mismatches execution TF judged PF; the sealed LLM rater agreed with the consensus at κ 0.903, secondary, D-84). The raters are the authors and saw the developer's fix; the audit still tests the labelling rule, which is mechanical and identical on both splits. Frame reconstructed; WSL2 instead of Docker; one replicate for E4/E5. Local compute is not priced: KUU used 8.0 and M1 DEB 13.6 GPU-seconds per D2 instance.

7b. Analyses added in response to review (D-112 to D-128; post hoc or exploratory)

Each was fixed in advance in the version-controlled decision log (docs/tracking/DECISIONS.md) before it was computed (internal provenance, not an external registration); none joins a Holm family or re-reads a confirmatory verdict. Full tables: paper Appendix G; tracking: docs/tracking/REVIEWER_RESPONSE_MATRIX.md.

  • Rates that do not depend on the class mix (D-113). TPR = PF kept / PF, TNR = TF removed / TF, balanced accuracy (BA) = their mean. On D2: KUU TPR 0.974, TNR 0.267, BA 0.621; M1 DEB BA 0.697; cascade BA 0.826. KUU is a safety rule: it keeps almost every bug-revealing test and removes a minority of test faults. At an assumed 5% PF prevalence it keeps 14.3 test faults per bug-revealing test kept (keep-all 19.0). A new figure (paper/figures/fig_tradeoff_rates.pdf) plots PF-loss rate against TF-removal rate on one scale for D1 and D2.
  • Ranking (D-114). AUROC on D2: KUU vote count 0.756, M1 DEB confidence 0.760, Sonnet DEB 0.970. Inspecting the top 20% finds more bug-revealing tests than random for every judge, but Sonnet's top 20% on D2 covers fewer bugs (0.45) than random inspection (0.51).
  • Trained baselines (D-115). Leave-one-project-out on D1-test: failure features alone AUROC 0.554 [0.394, 0.710]; the three votes, learned, BA 0.698 at TPR 0.883 (KUU: BA 0.594, TPR 0.983).
  • Cross-generation agreement (D-118), a zero-call signal from the buggy version only: AUROC 0.513 on D1, 0.708 on D2.
  • Risk-targeted threshold (D-117). Calibrated on D1 to a 5% PF-loss target, it realised 0.026 (KUU panel) and 0.065 (KUU-NG panel) on D2: not a guarantee, because D1 and D2 are not exchangeable and KUU was designed on D1.
  • Sample size (D-119). An MDE of 0.08 on DEB − SC needs about 257 bugs (4.3× the 60 now); planning guidance only.
  • Integrity (D-120). Holm reproduced in all 10 committed families; every interval resamples bugs; a frequentist GEE gives no specification effect (S0 OR 1.258 [0.896, 1.768]).
  • Leakage guard and label noise (D-121). The guard aborted 8 of 300 judged D2 instances, 5 on the injected line; relabelling up to 10% of TF assertion failures as PF leaves the order of the rules unchanged.
  • All held-out test faults judged (D-124). 290 D1-test test faults judged by M1, M2, M3 and Llama: KUU removes 0.23 of them and keeps 3.8 test faults per bug-revealing test kept at the natural prevalence (keep-all 4.8).
  • Greedy and repeated judges (D-125, D-127). M1's one-shot judge at temperature 0: BA 0.589, F1 0.430 (at the frozen 0.7: BA 0.588, F1 0.460). M1 DEB over 8 runs: F1 range 0.221, SD 0.076.

M1 with thinking on: BA 0.771, F1 0.693, MCC 0.513, 7 of 60 bug-revealing tests lost, 9 outputs cut off by the output cap; 6,800 tokens per instance against debate's 9,188 (not token-matched). 7c. Thinking-judge follow-up (D-130, D-131)

The thinking-enabled result above came from one exploratory run, so the authors had it tested the way KUU was tested (D-130): first its development gate, then a D2 confirmation with two hypotheses fixed in advance in the version-controlled decision log, before any D2 call, then two more D1-test runs. The wording rule for the paper was fixed in the same entry, before the result. Same configuration in every run: M1 J1 at S1, thinking on, context 16,384 tokens, output cap 8,192.

  • Development gate: failed. On the 55-instance dev pilot, valid outputs 96.8% (needs 98%; 2 cut off by the cap) and F1 0.312 against R0's 0.642 (thinking off: 0.350). The gate is a check, not a stopping rule, so D2 went ahead as fixed in D-130.
  • D2 confirmation: both hypotheses supported (270 instances, 64 bugs, one Holm family, thinking J1 against M1 DEB). HT-1: MCC 0.659 against 0.392, ΔMCC +0.267 [0.146, 0.391], p_Holm <0.001. HT-2: 8 against 56 bug-revealing tests lost, Δ −48 [−65, −32], p_Holm <0.001.
  • Output cap. 9 D2 outputs were cut off. Scoring them as keep instead of TF gives ΔMCC +0.253 and Δ lost −51; excluding them gives +0.259 and −50. The verdict does not depend on the coding.
  • Beside the other rules on D2 (descriptive; BA / TPR / TNR; PF tests lost). Thinking J1 0.810 / 0.948 / 0.672; 8. M1 DEB 0.697 / 0.636 / 0.759; 56. KUU 0.621 / 0.974 / 0.267; 4. Cascade 0.826 / 0.903 / 0.750; 15. Sonnet DEB 0.893 / 0.916 / 0.871; 13. KUU still keeps 6 bug-revealing tests the thinking judge loses (2 the reverse), so the thinking judge is a better classifier, not a safety rule.
  • D1-test, three runs (exploratory). BA 0.771–0.817, MCC 0.513–0.601, F1 0.693–0.742 (M1 DEB over eight runs: F1 0.393–0.614); PF tests lost 4–7 of 60.
  • Tokens. Prompt plus generated tokens, thinking included: 6,753 per D1-test instance (mean of the runs) and 6,356 per D2 instance, against debate's 9,188 on D1-test. The arm is not token-matched to debate; it happens to use fewer.
  • What changed in the paper (branch A of the D-130 rule). The planned-arm findings are scoped to "with thinking disabled" in the abstract, contributions and conclusion; the abstract and conclusion report the confirmed result with the gate failure beside it; the Discussion has a paragraph on test-time reasoning against orchestration; RQ4 says what KUU adds beyond the thinking judge. The claim is limited to M1's own planned arms, because the 12B M2's debate scored about as high on D1-test (MCC 0.571, inside the thinking range 0.513–0.601).
